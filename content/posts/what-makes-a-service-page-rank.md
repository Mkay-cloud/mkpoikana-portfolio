If you run a service business, your service pages are the most valuable pages you have. They are what shows up when someone searches "boiler installation in Calgary" or "commercial generator rental". Your homepage matters, your blog helps, but the service page is where search traffic turns into paying work.

Yet on most small business sites, these pages are the thinnest. A heading, two generic paragraphs, a stock photo and a contact button. Here is what I put on a service page instead, and why each part helps it rank and convert.

## One service, one page

The most common problem I see is one "Services" page listing everything the business does. It feels tidy, but it makes ranking almost impossible. Google tries to match a search with the single page that best answers it. A page that mentions ten services briefly is not the best answer for any of them.

Give each main service its own page. If you serve distinct areas, it can also make sense to have pages for key locations, as long as each one says something genuinely specific about that area rather than swapping the town name.

## Match the words people search

Look at how customers describe the service, then use those words where they count:

- The page title, which is what appears as the blue link in Google.
- The main heading at the top of the page.
- The first paragraph, so a visitor knows within seconds they are in the right place.

Write naturally. Repeating a phrase ten times does not help anymore and makes the page worse to read. One clear heading and honest, specific writing does more.

## Answer the questions buyers actually have

A page ranks well when it is the most useful answer to the search. For a service, that usually means answering what a buyer wants to know before they pick up the phone:

- What exactly is included, and what is not.
- Who it is for, and when it is the right choice.
- Roughly what it costs, or what the price depends on.
- How long it takes, and what happens step by step.
- Where you cover, and when you are available.

You do not have to publish an exact price list. Even a sentence like "most installations fall between X and Y, depending on these three things" is far more helpful than "contact us for a quote", and it filters out enquiries that were never a fit.

## Show proof, not adjectives

Every competitor says they are reliable, professional and friendly. Those words carry almost no weight. Proof does:

- Photos of real jobs, your real team and your real vans.
- Reviews that mention the specific service on the page.
- Certifications, warranties and memberships that customers recognise.
- A short case study: the problem, what you did, and what changed.

Proof helps people choose you. It also helps Google, which increasingly looks for signs of real experience and trustworthiness in the content it ranks.

## Make the next step obvious

A service page that ranks but does not convert is a wasted opportunity. Put the main action where people see it without scrolling, then repeat it after the key sections. For urgent services that means a tap-to-call button. For considered purchases, a short quote form usually works better than a long one.

Keep forms short. Name, contact details and one box for the details is often enough to start a conversation. You can ask the rest on the phone.

## Link it into the rest of the site

Service pages should not be islands. Link to them from the homepage and the main menu, from related services, and from blog articles that cover related questions. These internal links help visitors find the right page, and they help Google understand which pages on your site matter most.

## A quick check you can do today

Pick your most important service and search for it, including your town if you are local. Open the top three results and compare them honestly with your page. Which one answers more questions? Which one has more proof? Which one makes it easier to get in touch?
