Picture someone with water coming through their kitchen ceiling at eleven at night. They grab their phone, search "emergency plumber near me" and open the first few results. They are not going to read your About page. They want one thing: to talk to a person, right now.

If your site makes them hunt for a number, copy it, switch apps and paste it, a good share of them will simply press back and call the next result. That is the whole case for click-to-call, and it is why I treat it as the first thing to get right on any site for a business that runs on phone enquiries.

## What click-to-call actually is

It is a phone number on your website that starts a call when someone taps it on their phone. Technically it is a tiny change: the number is written as a special kind of link that phones understand. There is nothing to install and nothing to pay for.

The catch is that many sites show the number as plain text, or worse, as part of an image. On a phone, plain text sometimes gets detected and sometimes doesn't. An image never works. Either way the visitor has to do extra work at the exact moment you want it to be effortless.

## Where the number should go

Having a tappable number somewhere on the site is not enough. It needs to be where a stressed person's thumb lands first.

- In the header, visible without scrolling, on every page.
- As a clear button, not just a line of small text. "Call now" with the number next to it works well.
- Repeated at the end of each service page, where someone who has just read about a service is ready to act.
- On mobile, consider a slim bar fixed to the bottom of the screen with the call button, so it is always within reach while they scroll.

When I built the site for FlameTech Plumbing & Heating, this was the organising idea: emergency-first, with click-to-call above the fold for the calls that come in late at night. Every other design decision came after that one.

## Small details that make a real difference

Once the button exists, a few details decide whether it gets used.

Use a number that is actually answered. If calls after hours go to a voicemail nobody checks until morning, say so and offer the next best option, like a form that promises a reply by a set time.

Show your opening hours or "24/7" right next to the button if it applies. People hesitate to call if they are not sure anyone will pick up.

Make the button big enough to tap easily. Tiny links squeezed between other links cause mis-taps, and mis-taps cause frustration.

Keep the number consistent everywhere: on the site, on your Google Business Profile and on any directory listings. Mismatched numbers confuse customers and search engines alike.

## How to know if it is working

You should not have to guess. Google Analytics can record each tap on the call button as an event, so you can see how many calls your site starts each week and which pages they come from. If you run Google Ads, the same taps can be counted as conversions.

It is also worth asking new customers a simple question on the phone: "How did you find us?" It is low tech, but it catches the calls that analytics misses.

## Why this beats a redesign

Business owners often assume that more enquiries require a full new website. Sometimes they do. But I have lost count of the sites that look fine, rank reasonably, and still leak calls because the number is hard to use on a phone.

Fixing that is usually an afternoon of work. It does not change your branding, your content or your rankings. It just removes the friction at the one moment it matters most. If you only change one thing on your site this month, make it this.
