"Can you just do the SEO on it?" I hear some version of this on nearly every new project. It usually comes right after the design is signed off, as if SEO were a coat of paint you add at the end and then never think about again.

I understand why. A lot of agencies sell it that way: a one-off "SEO package" with a fixed price and a checklist. Some of that checklist is genuinely useful. But treating it as a one-time job is the main reason small business owners pay for SEO and then feel like nothing happened.

## What the one-time part actually is

There is a real setup phase, and it matters. When I build or rebuild a site, this is the part I handle once and do properly:

- A clean page structure, so every service has its own page instead of being crammed into one.
- Page titles and descriptions written for the searches people actually make.
- Fast loading, working mobile layout, and no broken links or missing pages.
- Google Search Console connected, a sitemap submitted, and the basics that tell Google what each page is about.

Think of this as building a shop with a good sign, a clear door and tidy shelves. It is necessary. It is also not the same as having customers walk in.

## Why rankings don't stay put

Search results are not a list that gets written once. They are recalculated constantly, and three things keep moving under you.

First, your competitors. The plumber two streets away is also publishing pages, collecting reviews and getting mentioned by local sites. If they keep improving and you stand still, you slide, even though nothing on your site changed.

Second, Google itself. It updates how it ranks pages many times a year, and a few times a year it makes larger changes that reshuffle whole industries. A page that was "good enough" last spring can quietly drop after one of these.

Third, your customers. The words people use change. New services appear, prices shift, and questions that nobody asked two years ago become common searches. A site written once slowly stops matching what people type.

## What ongoing SEO looks like in practice

For most small businesses it is not dramatic. It is a steady routine, done every month:

- Checking Search Console to see which searches you are showing up for, and which pages are slipping.
- Improving pages that are close to page one but not quite there. Often a clearer title, a better answer near the top, or a missing detail is enough.
- Adding a new page when you notice demand for something you offer but don't mention.
- Getting new reviews and keeping your Google Business Profile current.
- Fixing technical problems as they appear: a slow new image, a broken form, a page that accidentally got hidden from Google.

None of these tasks is big. Together, done consistently, they are what moves a business from page three to the map pack.

## How long before it works?

This is the honest answer most people don't want to hear: expect months, not weeks. In a local market with a few competitors, you can often see movement within two or three months of steady work. In a crowded market, or a brand new domain, it can take six months or more before the enquiries feel different.

That timeline is exactly why the one-time model disappoints. The setup is done in week one, the results would arrive in month four, and by then nobody is working on it anymore.

## So what should you pay for?

If you are hiring someone, I'd suggest thinking about it in two parts. A proper setup, which is a one-off cost and should be part of any decent website build. Then a smaller monthly amount of ongoing work, sized to how competitive your market is.

If the budget is tight, it is fine to do the ongoing part yourself. Block out an hour a month, open Search Console, and ask one question: which page is closest to doing well, and what would make it more useful? That habit beats any one-off package.
